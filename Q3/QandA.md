What are the layers of the Earth's atmosphere? ^^^The layers of the Earth's atmosphere are the Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, and Exosphere.



What are the characteristics of the Troposphere? ^^^The Troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere, where weather occurs, and temperature decreases with altitude.



What are the characteristics of the Stratosphere? ^^^The Stratosphere contains the ozone layer, temperature increases with altitude, and is where commercial jets fly.



What are the characteristics of the Mesosphere? ^^^The Mesosphere is where temperature decreases with altitude and is the layer where meteors burn up upon entering the atmosphere.



What are the characteristics of the Thermosphere? ^^^The Thermosphere is characterized by very high temperatures and is where the auroras occur.



What are the characteristics of the Exosphere? ^^^The Exosphere is the outermost layer of the atmosphere, where air is thin and satellites orbit the Earth.



What is a scaling factor? ^^^A scaling factor is a number that scales, or multiplies, a quantity to increase or decrease its size.



What is NASA's James Webb Space Telescope? ^^^NASA's James Webb Space Telescope is a space telescope designed to observe the universe in infrared wavelengths.



How is the JWST different from other telescopes? ^^^The JWST is optimized for infrared observation, has a larger mirror than previous telescopes, and can observe distant galaxies and exoplanets.



What are stars made of? ^^^Stars are primarily made of hydrogen and helium, with trace amounts of other elements.



What is the main chemical reaction that takes place in the center of stars? ^^^The main chemical reaction in stars is nuclear fusion, where hydrogen nuclei combine to form helium, releasing energy.

What is the electromagnetic spectrum (EMS)? ^^^The electromagnetic spectrum is the range of all types of electromagnetic radiation, from radio waves to gamma rays.



What are the types of radiation in the EMS? ^^^The types of radiation in the EMS include radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.



What is frequency in waves? ^^^Frequency is the number of waves that pass a point in one second, measured in hertz (Hz).



What is the difference between high and low frequency waves? ^^^High frequency waves have shorter wavelengths and more energy, while low frequency waves have longer wavelengths and less energy.



What is wavelength in waves? ^^^Wavelength is the distance between successive crests of a wave, typically measured in meters.



What is the life cycle of the Sun? ^^^The Sun's life cycle includes its formation from a nebula, its main sequence phase, expansion into a red giant, and eventual transformation into a white dwarf.



What are the parts of the Sun? ^^^The parts of the Sun include the core, radiative zone, convective zone, photosphere, chromosphere, and corona.



What is solar wind? ^^^Solar wind is a stream of charged particles released from the upper atmosphere of the Sun, primarily electrons and protons.



What are sunspots? ^^^Sunspots are temporary phenomena on the Sun's photosphere that appear as spots darker than the surrounding areas, caused by magnetic activity.



What are coronal mass ejections (CMEs)? ^^^CMEs are large expulsions of plasma and magnetic field from the Sun's corona that can affect space weather and Earth's magnetosphere.



What is NASA's Parker Solar Probe? ^^^NASA's Parker Solar Probe is a spacecraft designed to study the Sun's outer atmosphere and solar wind by flying close to the Sun.



How does Earth orbit the Sun? ^^^Earth orbits the Sun in an elliptical path due to the gravitational pull of the Sun, completing one orbit approximately every 365.25 days.



How does the Moon orbit Earth? ^^^The Moon orbits Earth in an elliptical path, completing one orbit approximately every 27.3 days, which causes phases of the Moon.



What causes the Earth's seasons? ^^^The Earth's seasons are caused by the tilt of its axis and its orbit around the Sun, affecting the angle and duration of sunlight received.



How does a lunar eclipse happen? ^^^A lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, causing the Earth's shadow to cover the Moon.
